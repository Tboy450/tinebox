## Physical work to establish a usable baseline

1. Print a compact tuning sample in standard PLA and/or PETG plus the bore-fit coupon. Measure actual thickness and free lengths. Keep recordings and note whether a stable fundamental is detectable.
2. Print one S2 calibration assembly. Inspect the carriage nose support and pick bay in the slicer. Establish smooth clearance and one clean pluck at low engagement, then measure C5/G5/C6 after clamping.
3. Correct the chosen material's lengths from those mounted results. Print one melody comb and compare the real recording with its ideal audition for note order and timing.
4. Print P1's single-peg rig, then swap to the tooth drum. Check contact loss, repeat plucks, torque, wear and tuning after mounting. Do not jump to a fully programmed drum before this works.

## Toward complete handheld designs

- S2: shorten the body around the chosen hook, retain cartridge access, add carriage retention/end limits, comfortable grips and a deliberate return action.
- P1: choose a note bank for the desired hook, develop multiple pin/tooth tracks and timing, then design drum retention and a handheld case.
- W1/T1: add the spindle/bearings and adjustable stationary pick, check that the carrier/core does not meet the pick, and test a short sector/one rotating tine before the complete rotor.
- Rotating tines transfer vibration through a moving rotor and support system, so the earlier stationary-comb soundboard approach cannot simply be assumed to work unchanged. Compare coupling and handling losses experimentally.
- Once tuning and plucking repeat reliably, test the soundboard/back/chamber variants in the internal acoustic-development notes. Fuller sound is still an unvalidated goal.

Keep a variety of concepts active, but complete and record one trial at a time. All new v0.1 materials remain local; the prior lab email was not changed or resent.
